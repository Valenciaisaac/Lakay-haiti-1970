01 · The setting

Haiti in 1970

Food is never just food. It reflects the land, the people who cultivate it, the history of migration and colonization, and the economic realities of everyday life.

INFLUENCE 01

African roots & Creole identity

Haitian cooking carries deep African culinary influence in its use of rice and beans, okra, roots, plantains, seasoning blends, stewing, and frying. These traditions developed into a distinct Haitian Creole food culture.

CultureFoodways
INFLUENCE 02

Colonial history & cultural exchange

Taíno, African, French, Spanish, and later other influences helped shape Haitian food. The menu's mix of cassava, local produce, seasoned meats, and French-influenced baked or sweet dishes reflects this layered history.

MigrationColonial legacy
INFLUENCE 03

Agriculture, trade & access

Rice, maize, beans, plantains, bananas, and sweet potatoes were important food crops. Coffee and sugarcane were major export crops. The balance between local harvests and imported goods affected what was available and affordable.

AgricultureEconomy
“A meal can tell the story of a people: what the land provides, what history has carried, and what families keep alive.”
A brief historical timeline
Before European colonization
Taíno communities cultivated and prepared foods from the island's plants and landscape, including cassava and local squash.
French colonial period
Plantation agriculture, enslavement, and the forced movement of Africans transformed the island's society and food system.
1804 · Independence
Haiti became independent. Food traditions continued to develop as expressions of Haitian identity and community.
1970 · The year of this experience
François Duvalier was president. He died in April 1971; his son Jean-Claude succeeded him. This was a period of authoritarian rule and significant social and economic pressures.

THE MARKET

Start with the harvest

Picture baskets of plantains, bananas, maize, beans, sweet potatoes, and greens arriving from local growers. The day's menu follows what is available rather than assuming every ingredient is always in stock.

THE KITCHEN

Cook with the tools of the time

The concept emphasizes fresh preparation, stovetop cooking, frying, simmering, and seasoning with herbs, citrus, and pepper. It does not assume modern refrigeration or modern convenience foods.

THE TABLE

Food made for sharing

Rice and beans, plantains, vegetables, and a main dish create a satisfying plate. Pikliz adds acidity and heat, balancing richer foods and giving the meal its unmistakable character.

Lakay means “home” in Haitian Creole. This fictional restaurant is a tribute to the cooks, farmers, and families who keep food traditions alive.
